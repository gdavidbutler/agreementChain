# agreementChain

Generated with Claude Code (https://claude.ai/code)

## What this is

A C89 library, [`ac.h`](ac.h) and [`ac.c`](ac.c), for one seat of n, n >= 3. Together the seats deliver the same chain of the seats' messages, in the same order, over UDP, while up to t of the seats are Byzantine, n > 3t: every correct seat that folds a link folds the same one. The network may lose, delay, reorder, replay or alter any datagram, and delivery is with probability 1 where its loss is fair, a link that never delivers being a partition, within each seat's retention.

Each link of the chain is one common subset: every seat proposes a message, the agreement decides which seats' messages are in, and the link is the hash of the link before it, that subset and every member's hash. A position is one link in the making, its agreement and everything run for it, numbered from 1. What the seats agree on is hashes. A message's bytes travel beside the agreement, encrypted, the ciphertext in shards and its key in shares, so that any threshold of its recipients can reassemble it, and every correct seat that folds the position reports each member's message as it assembles it.

The seat is a state machine with no I/O of its own: no socket, no thread, no clock, no randomness. The caller feeds it every datagram that arrives and the time, calls its pass on its own clock, sends the datagrams it hands back, gives it a message when it asks for one, and takes its reports through callbacks. `ac.h` states the contract. The head comment of `ac.c` is the description of the run, one seat's view from its dealt secrets to its reports. [`acTest.h`](acTest.h) holds the wire's names the library shares with its harness. This file says why things are the way they are.

## Where it sits

agreementChain is the top of a stack of repositories. Each but cryptoMule is its own repository, cloned beside this one:

- [asynchronousByzantineAgreementProtocols](https://github.com/gdavidbutler/asynchronousByzantineAgreementProtocols), the agreement library: Bracha's broadcast (`bracha87Fig1.h`) and BKR94's common subset (`bkr94acs.h`), which every link runs. Its papers assume each message comes from the seat that wrote it, and it leaves that to its caller.
- [cryptoMule](cryptoMule/README.md), a directory of this repository: the deterministic recipes that meet that assumption, an authenticated channel for a message handed straight over and message attribution for one another seat carries, and go on to forward secrecy and censorship resistance. `ac.c` implements them natively; cryptoMule's own programs are where they were settled, every message a file you can read.
- The primitives those recipes call: [ReedSolomonErasureCoding](https://github.com/gdavidbutler/ReedSolomonErasureCoding) for the shards, [ShamirSecretSharing](https://github.com/gdavidbutler/ShamirSecretSharing) for the shares of each message's key, both with Merkle trees, [MerkleLamportSignature](https://github.com/gdavidbutler/MerkleLamportSignature) for the signatures, and [canonicalHuffman](https://github.com/gdavidbutler/canonicalHuffman) to compress a message before it is encrypted.
- The hash suites, which the caller plugs in (`struct acHash` in `ac.h`), each with its HMAC and HKDF: [rmd128](https://github.com/gdavidbutler/rmd128), [rmd160](https://github.com/gdavidbutler/rmd160), [sha256](https://github.com/gdavidbutler/sha256) and [sha3](https://github.com/gdavidbutler/sha3). The library calls no hash of its own. The example deals rmd128 for the sign role and rmd160 for the kdf role; the harness takes any of them for the kdf role and any but rmd160 for the sign role. The sign role's width is what every agreed value and link rests on: two inputs with one hash take about 2^64 work at the example's 16 bytes, 2^128 at 32.
- In this repository beside the seat: the datagram framer ([`rsecFrm.h`](rsecFrm.h)), which carries each message as Reed-Solomon shards in authenticated datagrams, and its denial-of-service gate, halfsiphash ([`halfsiphash.h`](halfsiphash.h)), the SipHash reference implementation's, adapted to C89.

Read the agreement library's README first and cryptoMule's second: this file uses their words without explaining them again, and says only what a chain adds.

## Words

The [agreement library's README](https://github.com/gdavidbutler/asynchronousByzantineAgreementProtocols) defines a seat, a caller and the papers' words: Byzantine, Bracha's broadcast with its INITIAL, ECHO and READY messages (its readies), and BKR94's common subset. In brief, it adds: a sweep is one pass of a seat's retry over everything it has sent and still owes; BPR re-sends what a figure sent until the figure no longer owes it; patience is the sweeps a seat waits for all n before it goes on at n - t; the abandon is the sweep count at which a seat stops running, which this tree counts for each machine of a position; and the beside flag says that a caller's message rides beside a broadcast's value, and holds a seat's echo of the INITIAL until its caller has validated that message. [cryptoMule's README](cryptoMule/README.md) defines the apex, the sign and shard messages, the signing offset, the mask keys and their retention floor, and T, the threshold of a message's shares, which is not the agreement's t. This file adds its own:

- **link**: one value of the chain, the hash of the link before it, a common subset and every member's hash. The link before the first is the genesis, dealt to every seat.
- **position**: one link in the making, its agreement and everything run for it, numbered from 1. Its name on the wire is the link before it.
- **member**: a seat in a position's subset.
- **fold**: making the link, once the agreement has output its subset, or the seat has DISCOVERed it, and the seat holds a sign message of every member.
- **COMPLETE**: a seat having a position's subset, by its agreement's output or by DISCOVER; the fold follows.
- **payload, rotation, noop**: what a seat proposes at a position. A payload is its caller's message; a rotation moves the seat to the successor of its signing context; a noop is what it proposes with nothing to say.
- **the book**: every seat's current public signing key, and the successor its agreed rotation installs. It moves only at a fold.
- **unit**: what one recipient is sent of a member's message, its sign and shard messages.
- **row**: one message of an agreement or a broadcast on the wire. Its tag names it: its kind, its agreement, its broadcaster, its round, its sender and its position's name.
- **HOLDING**: the agreement library's annotation on an echo or a ready: its sender holds the broadcaster's INITIAL and what rode beside it, so the broadcaster stops re-sending them to it.
- **record, retention**: what a position leaves once its machines have reached their abandon, kept in a ring of the declared bytes, the retention, so that a seat behind can still be carried to it. `released` reports the ring letting a record go.
- **recover leg**: a broadcast from a seat holding a record to one seat whose row named it, carrying the seat's unit of a member and the position's description, its subset and every member's agreed value.
- **DISCOVER**: a seat taking a position's subset from T descriptions alike, from the recover legs of distinct seats, instead of from its own agreement.
- **partitioned**: a seat no other seat carries: alone, as every seat is from the deal until it hears the others, and for good behind the oldest record the others keep, where only its operator can carry it.
- **sign role, kdf role**: the two hash suites a seat is dealt. The sign role hashes what is agreed, signed and committed: the values, the signatures and keys, both Merkle trees and a payload's message hash. The kdf role makes the keystream, the masks, the key walk and the datagram authentication, through its HMAC, the keyed hash, and its HKDF, the key derivation built on it.
- **operator**: in the example and the harness, the stand-in for an application: it deals the secrets and starts the seats.

## Try it

```
make
./ac -A 100 4 1
make check
./acTest -W 2500000 -x 40 -n 2 -A 1200 4 1
```

The first is the example: four seats, one of them allowed to be Byzantine, each a process, each proposing two messages, and each prints its two reports as they come, sixteen chain reports and twenty-four message reports in all when every subset is full. Position 1 is every seat's rotation, so its chain report shows the reports' shape and the book moving: every member reads "rotation", and its link is the name every row of position 2 carries. Positions 2 and 3 are each seat's hello, the message the example proposes, one message report per member at every other seat, each carrying the text. Its last hello agreed, each seat calls the end, and position 4 is every seat's noop carrying it, the chain's last link: its chain report says "the end", and each seat stops on its own. Every seat prints the same chain. `-A` is the abandon, required, below: at least twice the longest agreement a rehearsal at the deployment's physics shows with one seat stopped, 100 sweeps here.

The second is the harness: the framer's own test, then thirty-five arms, each a run of the harness with its own faults and judge. The third is one arm by hand: two positions and the end, with seat 3 cut off both ways for forty ticks: it falls behind, and on its return the readies and units the others' seats keep re-sending carry it through all three, to the same reports and the same final hash they have.

`-W` is the bandwidth a seat sends, here 2.5 Mbit/s, so that four seats share 10 on the loopback; the example takes the same and defaults to it.

A seat's two reports are both in message hashes, given as the messages flow. The chain's, once an instance, at the fold:

```
previousChainHash:member1Hash:...:memberXHash:nextChainHash
```

with n - t <= X <= n members, which the example prints as each member's number and its hash. The messages', once a member, at that member's end at this seat:

```
previousChainHash:memberHash:message
```

or why there is no message. Beside them, the seat says when its retention lets a position's record go (`released`): a seat still behind that position is partitioned. Everything else in `ac.c` is recovery and physics, and what the seat counts of them is the application's instrument (`acCounters` in `ac.h`); a section below says what to read the reports and the counters for.

Two callers come with the library. [`example/ac.c`](example/ac.c) is the demonstration, an operator and its seats: it deals the pairwise secrets, the addresses and the genesis, launches each seat as a process over UDP sockets on the loopback, and each seat prints its reports as they come and, every thirty seconds and at its end, the counters an application reads to see whether its declaration fits. Each seat calls the end once its own messages are agreed, at whatever link that is there, and every correct seat that ends the chain ends it at the same link, below; each seat then runs its end, carrying a seat behind, and stops on its own, the operator saying nothing after the go. It takes the seats' count, the Byzantine bound and the abandon, which a deployment declares, below: a seat needs no other knob to run.

[`test/ac.c`](test/ac.c) is the harness: the same operator and seats, as threads of one process, with a declared network at each seat's send, a dealer that commits faults, cuts and partitions, and a judge over every seat's reports and counters. It binds a build of the library with its test interface on (`acTest.h`: the hooks, and knobs that stand where a seat derives), which a deployment never has.

Across a network the operator starts every seat itself, by a command its configuration names, whose standard input and output are the seat's link. The command is to be a secure channel, as ssh to the seat's host is. The deal, every pairwise secret among it, then crosses the network only inside that channel, and the seat at the other end is the one the operator started. The operator reads the link as bytes and cannot check either for itself.

The network is the operator's configuration, never something a seat reports: with address translation everywhere, the address a seat listens on is seldom the one another seat reaches it at, and two seats may reach a third at different addresses from where each sits, so the configuration says what each seat binds and, for every pair, where the one sends to reach the other. The deal carries those addresses as text, and each seat resolves them where it runs, since a name may resolve differently there. A seat sends to every address dealt for another, since it cannot tell which reaches it, and from any socket, since a datagram's source is nothing to its receiver: the sender is in the tag, proven by the datagram's authentication. The deal crosses machines as bytes, never as a system's own structures, since the seats may run on different systems.

Across a LAN, the operator on one host and one seat on each of the others, with a configuration of three lines a seat, what it binds, where every other seat reaches it, and how the operator starts it; the head of `example/ac.c` gives the configuration's format:

```
./ac -o lan.cfg -W 5000000 -L 2500 -A 28 9 2

bind 1 :43101
reach 1 192.168.1.36:43101
exec 1 ssh host1 'cd agreementChain && ./ac -l 2> seat.out'
```

The delay declared is not the network's: on a LAN it is under a millisecond one way. The tick is one feedback cycle at the declared physics, and a host answers at the end of its pass, so the delay that matters is the slowest honest seat's answer, which on a slow host is its pass, however long that takes. The abandon is twice the longest agreement of a rehearsal, the same deployment with one of the slowest hosts stopped long enough that agreements run with it silent from start to end; at a tick that covers the slowest answer the silence costs an agreement its patiences, and what the load adds beyond them is what the rehearsal measures. With a host silent, a tick shorter than the slowest answer costs more than it saves: every other seat re-sends it everything it owes at every tick, passes run past their tick, the agreements run longer, the chain slows, the host coming back is left out of more subsets, and its framer loses trains in reassembly that the re-sends then carry.

Across a WAN, one seat on each of two cloud hosts and the nine seats of the LAN above behind its router's address translation, each with a forwarded UDP port, the seats behind the translation reaching each other at their LAN addresses and every other seat reaching them through the router, the operator on the LAN, where ssh reaches every seat's host:

```
./ac -o wan.cfg -W 5000000 -L 2500 -A 28 -e 20 11 2

bind 3 :43101
reach 3 router:43101
reach 3 192.168.1.36:43101 from 2,4,5,6,7,8,9,10
exec 3 ssh host1 'cd agreementChain && ./ac -l 2> seat.out'
```

The delay is declared for the slowest hosts here too: the paths' delay is tens of milliseconds, and the slowest seat's answer is still its pass. Declared at the paths' delay the tick is short, and with every seat running the chain goes no faster, since a step goes on as its answers arrive; with a host stopped, the short tick costs what it costs on a LAN.

## The agreement library

This tree rests on the [agreement library](https://github.com/gdavidbutler/asynchronousByzantineAgreementProtocols) and uses its README's vocabulary unchanged: a seat, a caller, a sweep, patience, abandon, BPR, the beside flag; the abandon this tree counts for each machine of a position. Read it first. What it says, and what it says of the papers beneath it, is not said again here; this file says only what a chain adds. The library runs one common subset and says nothing of a next. A chain of them, what one link folds, how a seat that missed a link catches up, and what a seat may forget are this tree's. The one thing the chain adds to the library's wire, the name of a position, is the library's own tag widened by one element, as the section on the three windows says. One word comes from the library's headers: a seat says HOLDING of what it holds, on the messages it already sends. Recovery here is the exchange carried to one seat, as the section on the three windows says, and COMPLETE is a seat having a position's subset, from its own agreement on the others' rows or, whichever comes first, and where the others' agreements are gone the only way, from T descriptions alike beside their recover legs, after which it folds. That count is the chain's own: the library agrees values, never what rides beside one, as its README says under "What the papers assume", so what the others say their agreement decided is taken only where T distinct seats say it alike.

## Why a chain, and why a hash is agreed

Each seat's message is dispersed to the others by cryptoMule, whose recipes `ac.c` implements natively. Read [cryptoMule's README](cryptoMule/README.md) second, after the agreement library's: what a dispersal is, what it guarantees against whom, and why, are said there, and this file uses its words (the apex, the sign and shard messages, the signing offset, the mask keys and their retention floor) without explaining them. Two of its properties shape the chain. Any threshold of a message's recipients can reassemble it, so a seat the dealer censored assembles the message from the other recipients' exchange. And every pair's mask key ratchets forward on a chain link the seats must agree on, as its README says under Forward secrecy. The agreement fixes that value, and the link does two jobs at once, the next mask key of every pair and the linkage an external store keeps, which is why the chain is exposed rather than kept inside.

What is agreed is the apex, a hash, never the message. The agreement library carries its value in every echo and ready of every seat and bounds it at 256 bytes for that reason, so the dispersal's sign and shard messages, here a recipient's unit, ride beside the value's `(initial, v)` as the caller's message, under the library's beside flag. A seat echoes nothing of a dealer's `(initial, v)` without validating the unit beside it, and the library's README says why that keeps one value or none. Here it means a dealer that gives one seat a private apex and the others the real one is accepted at that seat under the value the others accepted, on their readies, or nowhere, and that seat holds the value and no unit, initiates no exchange leg for it, and takes the message from the others' legs. The harness deals that fraud on purpose (`-f 7`, `-f 8`) to see it refused and the message still delivered, and with `-f 10` the same apex signed at a second signing offset, which is a private apex too, since the value commits the offset.

Every value commits its signer and the signing offset it is signed at, as in cryptoMule, so one value is one kind under one key at one offset, whoever carries it. Beside cryptoMule's two kinds, a dispersal and a rotation, this tree has a third, the noop, below, and a rotation or a noop may carry the caller's end, a state of the seat and not a fourth kind, under "The end".

## The fold, the link and the book

The fold runs once the library has output the subset, which comes with every member's value accepted here, or the seat has DISCOVERed it, below, and never before: nothing acts on a value ahead of the agreement on it. It reads the member's kind and message hash from the sign message held under the member's agreed value, the dealer's or one brought by an exchange leg, and waits until one is held for every member. The link is the chain's head, the subset, and every member's hash, in subset order, hashed into one value: a payload's message hash, a rotation's or a noop's value, the hash column of the chain's report, so a reader of the reports folds the chain for itself. The subset is in it because two members may say the same bytes: without it, two subsets that differ by members who said alike would fold to one link, and the chain would not commit who said what, which is what an application counts when it trusts a message more than t distinct members sent. A payload's is its message hash and not its apex, as in cryptoMule's dispersal chain. It is in subset order because a common subset is a set and every seat must fold in one order. Every fold mints a link, a position of rotations or noops too, and the mask keys step on it (a cryptoMule rotation adds no link and steps no key), because the link is the next position's name on the wire and no two positions may share one.

A seat makes one proposal at each position, and a proposal is one of four things, in a fixed order: a rotation the last subset left out, presented again, the same value under the same signature, since its signing offset is one-time; else a payload a subset left out, presented again where the caller, asked first, gives the same bytes; else the rotation to a successor context it generates, where it has none made, or where its signing context is spent, after switching to the successor its agreed rotation installed; else a payload, the message the caller gives when asked, or, with nothing to say, a noop.

A signing context has 2^s signing offsets, offset 0 the rotation's, so position 1 is every seat's rotation and a context lasts 2^s positions. A rotation or a noop delivers no message: identity advances in the book, the messages' report says rotation or noop, and the chain and the mask keys step on every fold. The book, every seat's key and the successor its agreed rotation installed, moves only at the fold, so it moves at the same position at every seat: a member signed under its successor is promoted, a rotation member's successor installed. A seat left out of a subset loses nothing but a turn: a common subset need not hold every honest seat, and its caller, reading the chain report, may propose the same message again at the next position.

The payload is the message the caller gives when the seat asks for it (the propose callback), and nothing here queues one. That is the point: what became of a proposal is in its position's chain report, and only there can the caller decide what to propose next, the same bytes again if it likes, a choice a queue ahead of the agreement would already have made for it. The bound a message is refused past is the longest message cryptoMule's 64k ciphertext cap admits. The seat derives it and the application reads it back, since nothing the application knows fixes it, and a message is refused when it is asked, by its compressed stream, the noop proposed in its place, so no message asked fails later.

A position launches when the one in hand has folded here and this seat has a reason to: its caller says it has a message (`acAvailable`), or a rotation of its the last subset left out is presented again, or another seat's rows for it have arrived, held under the name the fold just minted. A common subset takes every seat's input to a position, so one seat's INITIAL makes every other seat take part, and a seat's noop is its input to a position it has nothing to say in. So one seat's message launches the position at every seat, the launch cadence is the callers', and an idle chain sends nothing. A seat that proposes at every fold costs every other seat a position of agreements, a payload signing offset of its context and a step of its live window, which each counts; a noop left out of a subset is let go, since agreeing it would buy nothing at that cost, and a payload left out is kept and presented again, the same value under the same signature and no signing offset spent, only where the caller, asked, gives the same bytes: its next turn is the caller's.

## Patience and phases

The agreement library's settings are the deployment's, and its README says why each exists. Here the seat derives both, and the harness alone overrides them (`-g` and `-P`). The phases an agreement may run are 85, the byte.

### Patience

The patience starts from two sweeps, the partial one a round starts in and the full tick after it, because the tick is one feedback cycle at the declared physics, as the section on the physics says, and an honest seat's row of a round arrives here within one while that seat's pass fits its tick, which sizes the patience and nothing else; a seat whose pass runs past it is late by what the pass carries, and where t allows, this seat enters 0 for it and goes on at n - t, so the slow seat's message waits for a later position. A sweep that re-sends is one that made no progress, so to those two the seat adds the sweeps the declared loss retries: for the steady loss, the sweeps until every row a wait reads, about three of every other seat's broadcast, has arrived ninety-nine times in a hundred; for the longest burst, every sweep it lasts. Each row is lost only with every datagram the parity sent it with, which the steady loss's count takes into account. Each is paid in the currency of FLP, the impossibility the library's README starts from: a member gone makes every patience run out in full, every instance; and an agreement that spends its phases undecided abandons its seat.

### Phases

The phases are not a bet. The coin is each seat's own, so once one agreement's inputs are split, its value's accept falling past patience at a few seats, and a scheduler goes on choosing what each seat samples, a phase ends that split only when more than (N + t) / 2 of the N - t honest coins fall the same way: one phase in four at four seats, one in sixteen at seven seats with two faulty, one in thirty-seven at twenty-six seats with five faulty, one in fifteen at forty-one seats with five faulty. The library's README gives the expected phases as a constant for t on the order of sqrt(N); the constant is good enough for a byte of rounds once 2t / sqrt(N - t) is 1.5 or under with N + t odd, N above about 1.8 t^2 + t: there a split agreement outlasts 85 phases less than once in a hundred thousand against a scheduler that sees every coin. The coins that end a phase are a whole count, more than (N + t) / 2, so an even N + t needs one coin more and its odds ripple a step behind. Nor need the network do the holding: the library's README shows the t faulty seats alone, where patience lets a round wait for all N and N is even, waiting for the correct seats' values and tying the full sample, so the phase ends on the coin again. That tie holds a split no more often than the adversary these figures are against, and at t = 1 exactly as often, since more than N / 2 honest coins alike break it, where the figures ask more than (N + t) / 2. The ceiling is paid in seats, never in phases: what the library's README calls the caller's bet on the phases is here the deployment's choice of N against t. And it is the whole chain's: the correct seats run the same figure to the same 85 phases, so where one reaches the ceiling undecided the others can, and a position none of them decides folds at none of them, and the chain stops there; where only some reach it undecided, their abandons together can pass t, which t cannot cover. A coin dealt ahead to every seat alike would trade the ceiling for a count, a supply of coins the chain can outrun.

What splits an agreement's inputs is timing, and without a split no phase reaches the coin. A seat's input to a seat's agreement is whether it accepted that seat's value before its patience ran out, a patience that starts once n - t agreements have decided 1 there. The agreement library holds the accept to one value at every correct seat, or none, and cryptoMule's recipes give it what it asks of the dealer and the wire: a row is its sender's word, so the network can delay a seat's word and never cast it; a value is one signed apex at one signing offset, the same bytes wherever it is accepted, and a correct dealer's unit passes at every correct seat; and a correct seat echoes a dealer's INITIAL only on a unit it validated, so a seat its dealer starves, or deals a unit that fails, is carried to the same accept by the others' echoes and readies and served its parts by their exchange legs. The inputs differ only where that one accept falls on either side of a seat's patience: a slow network, a scheduler, or a faulty dealer's own timing, its INITIAL sent or its units dealt so that the accept comes one hop later at some seats. The coin is the caller's randomness, local to the seat. And a message is unread below T shares, more than t, so a scheduler can split the agreement a message rides in, but never for what the message says, and a payload left out is presented again.

### The abandon

Every machine of a position, each broadcast the library runs for it, has the deployment's abandon for its count: the sweeps it runs before it stops (`abandon`), how long the deployment holds its memory. An abandon costs liveness, never safety: the papers' agreement properties count what correct seats sent and need no bound on their later silence, and at a position every seat has let go, records and DISCOVER stand where Lemma 3 did. The position's agreement counts it only once it has its subset, below. Until then it re-sends what it owes to the seats not recorded as having it, which is BPR: retry in the face of silence at the tick, the most that can be done about silence. Past the agreement's SUBSET the retry goes on at every sweep as before: what is still owed is owed to seats that have not said they hold it, and a silent seat may be a slow one, so nothing slows the retry on its silence; a re-send stops at its recipient's word or at the count, as the library's README says of BPR.

Where t allows a member gone, the seat refuses an abandon shorter than one agreement with that member silent, since the silence makes every round wait its patience in full: the launch's tick, the first phase of the agreements that decide 1, three rounds of a patience each, step 2's patience, and the first phase of those that decide 0, 1 + 7 patiences, 15 sweeps at a patience of 2 and 29 at 4; an abandon shorter than that ends every position at its agreement while one seat is silent. That is the least such an agreement can take, every round waiting exactly its patience, and the deployment's load makes it longer: the first decisions of 1, which it counts as the launch's tick, wait on the rows of every live position and on the re-sends toward the silent seat.

Nothing declared says what the load adds, so the abandon is not derived; it is required, and measured. Every seat counts the most sweeps an agreement of its ran to its SUBSET and the agreements it abandoned at the round ceiling with no subset (`acCounters`); short of the ceiling no agreement is abandoned before its subset, so a seat returning keeps its agreement at the positions the others decided without it until recovery, below, gives it the subset. Rehearse the deployment under its load with one seat stopped, and declare the abandon at twice the longest agreement the counters show, the least a rehearsal says, since a seat returning loads the others past it; a position a running seat DISCOVERed in the rehearsal (`recovered`) says the abandon is short, since the others let their agreements go before that seat had the subset, and a seat cannot tell itself returning from slow; in the deployment it says the abandon is short of what the deployment did: one seat more silent than it allows for, or a seat returning that loaded the others past it.

Above, the abandon is bounded by what it costs. A position's machines live until their count, so the positions live at once are about the abandon over the sweeps between launches, each its agreement's machines, its legs and its units: that, beside the retention, must fit the host's memory. The sweeps between launches are the deployment's at full speed, not the rehearsal's: an abandon measured with a seat stopped, where an agreement runs its patiences out, is many positions when every seat is present and the positions come fast. And a seat returning launches faster still, as it walks forward by recovery, below, and holds live every position it launches within one abandon of its own sweeps; its pass carries every one of them, so its sweeps stretch with what it holds, and one abandon of them covers more positions still. Nothing declared bounds that. The memory to fit is the returning seat's, and a seat that returns is the rehearsal's harder case. The library's seat of one position's agreement is 1.8 MB at eleven seats and 255 rounds, as its header computes, before this seat's own tables beside it. And while a seat is silent, every live position re-sends it what it owes, units among it, at every sweep until its count, so the tick must carry that beside the positions' own rows, or the next position waits behind them. Where no abandon is both above the floor and inside those bounds, the declaration does not fit the deployment: more bandwidth, a longer tick or smaller messages, never a shorter abandon.

Short of the round ceiling, a position's agreement is never abandoned before its subset is had, its own SUBSET or one DISCOVERed: the agreement library is given no count for it, and this seat lets it go at the abandon count only once it has the subset, which is the memory the deployment holds.

A seat's state from the deal on is alone, which is the same state as partitioned, and nothing coordinates when seats show up: a seat alone at the deal, or cut off, keeps re-sending its same rows under the same tag, and when others show up they answer it as a slow seat. An agreement started again would forget what it had said and could say something else under the same tag; every seat silent past the abandon would then start its agreement again and be such a one, past t, and the chain could fold two subsets at one position: the restart, not the silence, is the danger. A seat whose agreement has its subset but whose fold still lacks a member's sign part runs its own value broadcast on alone, the same bytes under the same tags, and a position the others folded comes to either by recovery, below: the name in the tag is a past link to a seat that has folded past it, and that starts the recover legs that give it the subset it missed. Three arms show the re-send: the whole network dark, from the go and mid-chain, and a dealer that sends one seat nothing of its own with the exchange toward that seat dark; in each, every seat completes the position once what it is owed can reach it again, inside the abandon. Two show the agreement outlasting its abandon count: the whole network dark past it, and one seat dark past it while the others go on; in each, every seat folds every position.

### The tick

A sweep is a pass of the seat's one retry cursor over every live position, and the tick that starts a pass is where the physics lands: BPR has no round trip of its own, so the seat derives the tick from the declared bandwidth and delay as one feedback cycle: the longest step a seat says at once, an INITIAL with its sign message beside it to every other seat, paced at the bandwidth, the delay there, an echo back from each, and the delay back. The tick is a clock, not a budget. A pass carries what the sweep owes, every instance of every live position, a payload's shards among it, paced at the bandwidth; a pass that carries more than the tick runs past it, and the next pass starts when it ends, which is how the tick follows the load without a declaration of it, and patience and the abandon, counted in sweeps, stretch with it. A position launched under a tick before is passed over: the tick is one feedback cycle, so nothing it said can have drawn its answer yet, and a re-send then is load and nothing else. A tick that budgeted a whole position's pass would be long for the wrong reason, the load, which the pass already follows; the tick is long only for the slowest answer, and a long one costs a running chain little, since a step goes on as its answers arrive and the tick paces only the re-sends and patience. The harness's `-r` stands in for the derivation.

## Behind, and the three windows

A seat whose agreement has not completed while the others folded is behind, and it cannot tell by how much: a dead seat and a slow one look the same, forever, as the library's README says, so another seat's silence is not a fact. It does nothing new. Its agreement seat keeps re-sending what it owes, its own proposal among it, the same bytes under the same tags, and the others' agreement seats at that position, live until their windows let it go, keep re-sending what it has not acknowledged; that alone carries it to the same subset, which is the library's own guarantee, and where the others' machines there have reached their counts, the descriptions beside their recover legs, below, carry it there instead. A seat that folded a position never agrees it again, and the returning seat's agreement there is never started again either: a fresh one could say something other than it had said under the same tags, and a re-run with a member now silent, or an honest proposal that has since arrived, could close on another subset; the chain would fold two, and the keys would diverge silently, the one failure the construction exists to prevent.

### The name is the chain

Every row on the wire names its position, and the name is the chain. The library tags every row with its agreement, its broadcaster and its round, because one common subset keeps n agreements of n broadcasts alive at once; a chain keeps several common subsets alive at once the same way, so its tag is one element wider. That element is the chain hash before the position, the link the fold before it minted, the dealt genesis for position 1: every seat that folded the position before holds it, no seat holds it earlier, and the reports publish it, so it carries no secret. It is not a number, because a number is a counter with a wrap and the chain runs for no bounded length, and not a byte of one, because a byte names a position only within a window of its period and needs the window to place it. A seat routes a row to the position whose name it minted itself, holds a row under a name it has not minted until it does, as the library stores a row of a round it has not reached, and reads the rows it held as arrivals when the position launches, one a call, as `acInput` takes one datagram, so a returning seat holding thousands reads its socket between them instead of leaving it to overflow; a row held under the name a fold just minted is a reason to launch the next position; so is a row under a name not yet minted, once, at its arrival: its sender is at a position past this seat's head, and a seat that re-sends the same rows, one gone past the retention among them, launches nothing more, where a seat ahead sends rows under new names as it goes.

A held row is found by its name and by its shape, and a record by its name, in balanced trees whose nodes lie in the rows and in each record's index entry, so that a lookup is logarithmic in what is held whatever names a sender chooses: a seat behind holds tens of thousands of rows, and a search over all of them at every arrival is a cost a slow host cannot pay, as the section on the physics says. What is held is bounded by the shape of a position and the live window, not by what a sender says: one row of a shape per sender per name, and, per sender and shape, as many names as the live window holds and one more, for the record after the one a seat behind last named, since a sender sends for its live window and no more; a row past that is its sweep's to bring again.

The name is the sender's word, unsigned: a lie in it misroutes the liar's own row into a machine where the figure counts that sender once. cryptoMule carries no link, since a seat can recompute it; here several positions are live at once and a seat must route a row before it can recompute anything, so the link rides. The noop is this tree's own too: a seat with nothing to say still spends its next signing offset, on a value naming its key and that offset alone, so that its signing context keeps the chain's cadence.

### Recovery

What brings a seat behind to COMPLETE is what every seat already sends, and one Figure 1 more. Every communication here is one of the library's broadcast machines: the agreement's broadcasts, the exchange legs, which carry a seat's unit of each member to every seat, and the recover legs, below. A machine re-sends to the seats not recorded as holding until its count, the deployment's abandon; a seat that received nothing is one of them, and its silence is what a slow seat and a dead one both look like, so nothing is launched on silence and nothing is let go on it. Completion is the seat's own agreement on the others' rows, the library's guarantee again, or, whichever comes first, the others' word on what their agreements decided, beside their recover legs. Nothing asks: a recover leg starts on a row the returning seat sends anyway, so a seat heard by nobody is recovered by nobody: what it is owed it is owed by the machines still running, and the one-sided arm, a seat cut both ways and healed on ingress first, shows that edge.

### The windows

What a seat may forget is decided by the deployment's counts and by memory, by age alone. A position's machines run until each reaches its count, the deployment's abandon: that is the first window, participation, and what it lets go is the machines, whose rounds an agreement needs while it runs and no position needs once it has folded. A position folded here whose machines have all reached their counts leaves a record in the retention (the retention parameter, `-M` in the harness): its subset, every member's agreed value, the book as it stood at the launch, this seat's unit of each member and its own distribution, keyed by the position's name, in a ring of the declared bytes, the newest record overwriting the oldest, so nothing decides when to evict. That is the second window, recovery, and what it keeps is what recovery needs, which is not the machines but what they would send.

### Recover legs

A row from another seat naming a recorded position is evidence, and the question is the one a live row asks: does this seat have a machine to deal with it? A recover leg's own row is fed to its machine. Any other row is its sender's, and where no recover leg of this seat's runs toward that seat at the position, this seat starts them: for every member whose unit it holds under the member's agreed value, its own among them, one of the library's Figure 1 at n = 2 and t = 0, this seat initiating and the sender the other process, the member's agreed value its v, and beside it this seat's unit of the member and the position's description. It is the exchange carried to one seat; the many-to-many exchange's legs carry no description, since only a seat that missed the agreement needs to be told what it decided. From then on they are BPR's machines like any leg, re-sent to the returner alone until it says HOLDING, the unit and the description held, or until the abandon, and the next row naming the position after that starts them again, the same value and the same bytes beside it, so a fresh one says what the first did.

A seat that has folded the position opens a recipient on the INITIAL as any seat does and says HOLDING, so a recover leg toward a seat that holds the position retires like any other, and a recover leg's own rows start nothing. What an abandoned machine received goes with it, and nothing the record holds. A part of a member's distribution to this seat landing after the record was written goes into the ring too, as a newer copy of the record at the head, so the ring alone holds what a recover sends; the first record keeps the position's place in the ring's order, which the key floor steps in, and its overwriting is the position's let-go. The newest record overwriting the oldest at the retention's tail is this seat's abandon of the position, and the only thing it forgets. Nothing here reads where another seat is: a row names a position, and the machines for that position answer it.

### DISCOVER

What a recover leg does not start again is the agreement, and nothing here re-runs one. A position the others folded has its subset fixed by their agreements, and the returning seat needs that subset and its members' values, not a consensus of its own: recovery has no consensus to reach. The description beside a recover leg is the subset's bitmap and every member's agreed value in seat order, the same bytes beside every recover leg one seat initiates. A seat checks a description by its shape and against what it holds itself, its subset where it has one and the values its own value broadcasts accepted, which still answer at a seat whose agreement never reached its subset, the library's own reading. One description proves nothing, as the library's README says: agreed is not true, and a faulty seat can describe the same lie to every seat. So a seat whose agreement there has not completed DISCOVERs the subset at T descriptions alike from the recover legs of distinct seats, T being the secret-sharing threshold, which the seat derives above t: at most t seats are faulty, so one of the T is a correct seat's, and correct seats all describe the one subset their agreements decided. There are enough of them: the correct seats other than the returning one that folded the position and still hold its record number at least n - t - 1 where every correct seat folded it within its retention, and that is at least T at n > 3t and n >= 3. A description is no Figure 1 value, so the count is of distinct senders, the in-tag sender the dealt tier verified. The recover legs that bring a member's units to the threshold of assembly bring the descriptions with them, and a seat's recover leg of its own value carries its own distribution's unit to the returning seat, so a member's units come from its dealer too and not only from the seats that held them. The returning seat folds once it also holds a sign message under every member's value, mints the next name, launches the next position on its caller's word, a rotation presented again, or the rows it holds under the new name, and so walks forward a position a round trip; its own machines at a position it DISCOVERed run on to their counts, and each of their rows that lands where the position is recorded is evidence there, so the recover legs it still lacks keep coming after it has moved on. Its exchange legs there, launched at its fold, go to every seat, and a seat that has recorded the position holds nothing for them, so they re-send to it until their count: the cost of a late fold, paid by the seat that folded late.

### The returning seat

The others, meanwhile, wait their patience on the returning seat at every round of theirs, since it is silent at their positions, and that slows the frontier to about the returning seat's own pace. That wait is what lets a seat behind catch up at all: no evidence a seat has may skip it, since a seat coming back looks like one merely slow, and a frontier that did not wait would run on at its full pace and leave the returning seat behind the oldest record. What closes the gap is the returning seat's own side. A position it DISCOVERs waits no patience at all. At positions whose machines still run at the others, those machines' re-sends carry it instead. A seat with nothing to say, whose head position folded with every holder recorded and whose next left the others' windows before any row of it arrived, says nothing of its own; the others' live rows, under names it has not minted, are its reason to launch the next position, whose rows then start the others' recover legs toward it from their records.

A seat behind the oldest record is the third window, partitioned until the operator intervenes, and it is the operator's to see, from the chain the reports publish: a row under a name a seat has neither minted nor kept is one it cannot place, and no seat reads another seat's position from anything but the rows it is given. The mask keys follow the two windows: cryptoMule's current step is the oldest live position's, its retention floor the oldest record's, each stepped as its window lets a position go, so forward secrecy starts at the oldest record. Nothing a seat holds is let go on account of what the other seats say or do not say, but a seat's agreement at a position, once T of them say alike what every correct seat's agreement there decided.

Before a position leaves the live window, every member there without a verdict gets the last pass of the search that assembles messages from the units held, and its report, but a member still under threshold, or whose last pass this seat's memory refused, whose assembly its record keeps. A returning seat pays its own way: it proposes at every position it recovers, each complete elsewhere, and the units stream to it at the pace of the others' legs, so a position can leave its live window with a member still under threshold. That member's assembly goes with the record: a part of it landing later is a message received all the same, as Bracha's processes count "those received at previous steps", and the record places it, through the same gates; nothing is sent for it, since under unbounded latency a part that has not landed is one that has not landed yet. The report says under threshold only when the retention lets the record go. A position of four seats with messages of two hundred bytes, every seat in the subset, leaves a record of about eighteen kilobytes, four signatures of four kilobytes being most of it, so the default retention of sixteen mebibytes holds about nine hundred of them; what tens of hours hold at a membership is the operator's to declare, from the longest record a position can leave, which the seat derives and refuses a retention below, and the messages it expects.

How wide a window a returning seat needs is arithmetic: every other seat's legs stream it a unit of every member at once, about N signatures each, so its ingress takes (N - 1)^2 signatures a position; at four seats that is tens of kilobytes a position, at twenty-six seats it is megabytes, seconds of a congested ingress, which the returning seat's own counters say, the members its records kept and how many ticks after the record their parts land rising while the others' counts of rows naming a retained position rise, and the abandon is where the operator answers. A recover leg re-sends to its returner alone, a dead one among them, until the abandon, and each row naming the record after that starts it again: a seat whose rows name no record costs nothing there, and one whose rows do costs each holder an abandon of re-sends to it a row, which no other seat pays.

## The end

When a chain ends is its application's decision, made out of band. What the library supplies is that every correct seat that ends the chain ends it at the same link, and none ends it unless a correct seat's caller asked: no one seat can be trusted to choose. A local stop cannot give that. A seat that stopped launching at its own count leaves the others free to fold the next position at n - t, the faulty seats helping, and correct seats' final hashes become prefixes of each other, where choosing one would be another agreement. The command cannot be a message either, since a payload's bytes are assembled after later links may have folded, at a different link at each seat; nor a broadcast of its own, which every correct seat accepts or none does, but each at a link of its own. It has to be read at the fold, where every correct seat that folds a position holds the same subset and the same sign messages.

So the end is a state of the seat, not a message (`acEnd`). Every value the seat signs from the call carries it, a byte last in the value's preimage and in its sign message's shape, so the link commits it through the value and no seat can read one value both ways. Those values are rotations and noops, since the caller is asked for no more messages. The end is not a fourth kind: signing offset 0 is the rotation's alone, so a context spent must rotate, and a seat alternating a plain rotation with an end value would let a scheduler pick subsets that never reach the count. A value signed before the call is presented again as it was, its signing offset being one-time; a payload a subset left out is let go, the caller's to propose again in a next chain, which it knows from what it proposed and the subsets. The call is a reason to launch, as a message is, so an idle chain folds on to its end.

The chain ends at the first fold whose subset holds more than t members carrying the end, counted in that one subset. t faulty seats cannot reach the count, so a correct seat's caller asked. A subset guarantees only n - 2t correct members, a scheduler being free to keep any one correct seat out of every subset, so counting across links would buy nothing; once every correct seat's caller has called and its one value signed before has landed or been let go, n - 2t > t at n > 3t, so the count is reached. A larger count would be a vote, and the application has voted. Nothing launches after the last link, by any path, and its link is the final hash. A next chain may take it as its genesis, the hash more than t seats report alike, so a correct seat's, and is dealt afresh: no seat's identity outlives its process, so a seat that crashed has none to come back with, and the framer authenticates a datagram by its pair secret alone, so under the old secrets what the old chain put on the wire, replayed by the network or sent by a seat still running in it, would be a seat's word in the new one.

The seat stops on its own, never on another seat's word. It runs its last link to its abandon count and holds until every member of it is reported here: a seat without a message the others delivered is slow, not stopped, as at a launch. Then, for one abandon more, the last link's record carries a seat behind as any record does, so a seat cut off across the ending link learns it from the others' recover legs, folds it, and ends at the same final hash. After that nothing new starts, whatever a row names, so no faulty seat can keep a seat alive; machines already running go on to their counts, and the seat goes quiet, its framer drained, `acWork` returning none. A seat still behind then is in the third window. `acQuiet` stays the caller's local stop, a silence to the others, one of t.

## The loop, the framer and the physics

Everything a seat decides is on its own state, so the seat owns no thread, no socket and no clock: the caller's loop hands it a datagram and the time (`acInput`), or the time alone (`acWork`), and the seat hands back the datagrams due and how long until the next is. One owner of all the state needs no lock and no channel, and a seat that does no I/O runs the same under a process a socket, a thread a socket, or one loop over many; it is also how the agreement library is shaped, and this one keeps the shape. The seat's datagram framer, `rsecFrm.h`, is inside the seat and shaped the same way: the seat hands it a message and the time, and asks it for the datagram due. Its pacing is a due time, which comes out of `acWork` as the wait, and a datagram the caller's socket will not take stays in the framer until the caller calls the pass again. Its test stands alone: it derives every datagram it expects from the wire format `rsecFrm.h` states, and judges every delivery against what it sent.

### Bandwidth

UDP has no back pressure. Nothing tells a sender the network is full; a datagram past the network's capacity is simply lost, and a sender that outruns it loses its own traffic and every other socket's on the way. So a deployment states its bandwidth, a parameter in the bits the wire carries, IP and UDP headers among them, and every seat paces its datagrams to it, one datagram's wire time per shard. There is no unpaced seat. The harness's loss, delay and bursts are applied at each seat's send, where the caller owns the wire. A loss in the kernel, a full receive buffer, is fair loss to BPR like any other; a run that shows one is sending harder than its physics carries, and the remedy is to send less.

The bandwidth is declared at each seat's send, but it is spent at a receiver too: a seat behind is owed everything for the others' whole live windows, so N - 1 seats re-send to it at once, N - 1 times the declared bandwidth, and the declaration must fit what the slowest host can take in, its processor and its socket's receive buffer, not only its link. A seat behind on a host that takes in less than the others can send it loses datagrams in its kernel, and with no loss declared no parity carries a message across the gaps; what was dropped is the senders' sweeps' to bring again, by their live machines or, past their windows, by the recover legs their records start, so within the retention it comes back all the same: the kernel's count shows what was lost, and the seat's own counters the cost, its passes past their tick.

### Memory

Memory is physics as the network is, and a seat that cannot allocate is a slow seat, never a stopped one: a seat that stopped on a shortage would be a fault paid out of t, and a seat cannot know a shortage is not passing. A row it cannot allocate for is refused, as a lost one, and its sender's sweep brings it again; a refusal of this seat's memory is never counted against the seat that sent the row. A step of its own, a launch, a leg, a record, spends nothing until its memory is had and waits for the seat's next call: a launch takes no number, spends no signing offset, makes no successor and asks its caller only once, keeping the message while it waits. An assembly's search waits for the next tick, past its position's end with the record, and holds the next launch, or the seat's end, as a member short of parts does; only where the retention lets the record go first is the member reported with no verdict.

Two runs by hand refuse one seat's memory its search of a member until the position's end keeps the member, one at the search's buffers and one at its pool's, with the member's shard landing after the end: each position's launch waits on it, and the member's message is reported there as at every other seat. A library's refusal that may be its own memory, a signature or a coding through the hash suite's allocator, waits the same way. Only a library whose sole refusal is a parameter, refusing one the derivation validated, stops a seat (`acFailed`), which no deployment reaches.

### The tag and the talk

A tag is unique among the messages in flight. A re-send leaves under the tag of the message it repeats, the same bytes, and while that message is still in the framer's table the re-send is that message, still going: a second train under one tag would feed one reassembly at the receiver, and two messages would be delivered as one. The framer says so, and the seat takes the re-send as said.

The gate refuses a datagram from outside the deal, but a seat inside it passes every gate, and what it sends costs whoever it sends to. The agreement library bounds what a seat can be made to say, one reply for one arrival, back to its sender, and leaves the order of a bounded egress to the caller. A seat that said every reply before its own word would let one faulty seat, sending faster than it can answer, hold its whole egress: nothing it owes would be said and its pass would never step, so its patience and its abandon, counted in sweeps, would stand still, and a faulty seat that also withheld its own rows would keep it waiting for all n forever.

So the talk is an elevator over the seats, a stop each: at another seat's stop a reply owed to it, at its own stop its own word, what its instances owe, then its pass, then a new link's value broadcast. The car takes one turn at a stop that has one and moves on, and passes a stop with none. A seat that floods fills its own stop and no other, and the seat's own word waits at most one turn at each other stop. A rate limit is the usual answer, and it is not here: it would be a count with no physics to derive it from, and it would hold a reply back with the wire idle, turning it into its sender's re-send a sweep later. The elevator has no count and no clock and leaves the wire idle only when no stop has a turn, and the asynchronous model lets a reply wait: a wait costs latency, and a reply still waiting at its sender's next sweep draws that sweep's re-send, which is owed the one reply already owed. A reply owed is a bit by its seat and the row it answers, so a seat that repeats itself is owed once, and all it can be owed is bounded by the rows it can name, not by how fast it sends.

The same reading holds on the way in. The framer's table of messages in reassembly is shared among the senders the gate proved: a new message on a full table evicts the least recently fed of the sender holding the most, so a seat that opens messages it never finishes evicts its own. And a sign part whose value is not the one it must be is refused before its signature is checked, a hash where a check would be. What no seat can answer from inside is a flood that fills its link or its socket: the gate saves its processor, not its wire.

### Reconciliation

The harness's reconciliation counts every datagram. Its stop has two steps: at the first every seat stops talking, sends what its framer still holds at its pace, and answers; when every seat has, at the second each counts what is still unread in its socket and sends its counters. So every datagram a seat's send took was received, was in flight in a socket at the stop, or was lost in the kernel, and a run with nothing in flight and nothing lost reconciles message for message.

## What the library leaves to its caller, and this program supplies

The agreement library's README names the four clauses of the model it runs on: "no messages are lost" it supplies itself, BPR over a fair-loss network, here with the parity shards of each message sized from the declared loss; the other three it leaves to the caller. Here "no messages are generated" and "can identify the sender" are cryptoMule's authenticated channel made per datagram, the datagram framer's authentication tier, with a denial-of-service gate ahead of it (halfsiphash, the SipHash reference implementation's, adapted to C89), each keyed, as the mask key is, by its own slice of the one secret dealt to each pair; identity is the in-tag sender that tier verified, never a source address. The operator deals the secrets and the addresses; each seat mints its own signing context and publishes the public key alone; nothing is ever on disk. That each seat can send to every other is the operator's configuration, which deals, for every pair, where the one reaches the other.

## What a deployment can see

What the reports deliver is agreed, and agreed is not true, as the library's README says: every member's message reaches every correct seat that folds the position byte-identical, within that seat's retention and memory, a faulty member's as much as a correct one's, or reaches none, and up to t of a position's members may be faulty. Its parts are there to reach them: under the beside flag the first correct echoes of a member's value come on its INITIAL, after that seat validated the unit beside it, and the first correct ready needs more than (n + t) / 2 echoes, so before any correct seat accepts the value more than (n - t) / 2 correct seats, at least T, hold a valid unit, and each, once it folds, sends its parts to every seat by its exchange legs. A member's message reaches none where its commitment is not its encoding, which cryptoMule's re-encode after a PASS finds: from such a commitment, which seats reproduce the signed hash is which parts the recipients forward them, and a faulty recipient that gives its part to one seat alone would give that seat alone the message, so no seat delivers it, and every seat that reproduces the hash finds the same commitment inconsistent. Which verdict a seat reports for such a member is which parts reached it: the commitment inconsistent where a subset reproduced the hash, every subset tried where none did, the try budget spent where the parts held made more subsets than it allows. Two correct seats may report different ones, and what is the same at both is the outcome, the message in the same bytes or none, so an application acts on that and never on which none.

A message is never missing at one correct seat because it was inconsistent at another: a commitment that is the encoding of a message no longer than a proposal admits has no part off it, every part being checked against it on arrival, so any threshold of parts gives the message.

One seat can still miss a message the others have, under threshold when its retention let the record go, or its memory refusing the search then. That seat was outside what its deployment declared, one of the t there, and the library gives it the message no more within the chain. It still folds the chain, though, and its chain report carries the member's agreed hash, so its application can take the bytes from any other seat's application, by any means of its own, and keep them only if they reproduce that hash: no source need be trusted, and nothing is added between the seats. A seat that cannot follow the chain at all, behind the oldest record or crashed, comes back in the next chain, from the end's final hash under a fresh deal.

An application that must trust what a message says trusts it only when more than t distinct members sent it alike; the subset holds at least n - 2t correct members' messages, room for that count to pass where they agree, and every seat counts over the same messages. The chain is the order of what was said, not a judgment of it.

A chain of agreements costs more than a leader's log: every position is N agreements, every message is signed, shared and sharded to every seat, and a seat re-sends what it owes for the live window, and from the record whenever a row names the position, until retention lets it go. An application takes that cost for a network it cannot trust, and it should be able to see, from the seat's own reports and counters, whether the declaration it runs under fits the network it has and whether that network is ever doing what the construction is built for. The two reports carry part of that and the seat's counters the rest; the harness prints and judges both at its stop, and an application reads the same through `acCounters`.

The declaration is the physics, bandwidth, loss, burst and delay, and the datagram, and each but the burst has a witness: a burst is silence, which no seat can tell from a slow peer. The parity shards a message leaves with are sized from the declared loss, and the seat counts the parity shards its decodes used, the most one message used, and the decodes that used every shard the message left with: such a decode was one loss from an eviction, which BPR then pays for with a re-send a tick later, so a declaration that fits shows a few of those and one that does not shows many. A send the caller's socket refused is a bandwidth stated above what the socket carries, and the seat counts those. The tick is one step's feedback cycle; the seat counts the passes that ended past the tick they were due in, which is the live positions' load stretching the pass, and keeps the longest a tick started past its due, which is the same pass run long, or a caller's loop that calls past the tick's due. The datagram tiers' refusals are counted too, a datagram under no dealt key and one not from the sender its tag names: the deployment's view of the model's "no messages are generated".

Patience is the sweeps a seat waits for all n before going on at n - t, and the chain report says, at every position, what this seat entered in each seat's agreement: 1 where it held that seat's value by then, 0 where its patience ran out first. A 0 for a seat that is in the subset all the same says this seat was the slow one, not the member; a 0 at every seat for one member, position after position, is a member gone, and the subset's size against n is the Byzantine bound being spent on it. The message report's bitmaps of the parts each recipient held, against the secret-sharing threshold, say how much margin the exchange had.

Reaching the coin is the reading an operator deploys for. The agreement beneath is deterministic until a phase ends in doubt, and only there does it take the caller's coin, which is the one place a deterministic protocol must give way to chance under FLP; a first phase ends in doubt only when one value's accept fell past patience at some correct seats and not others, a split, which a slow network, a scheduler or a faulty dealer's own timing makes alike, and a later one also where the t faulty seats tie a full sample, as "Patience and phases" says. So the chain report carries, for every seat, the round its agreement decided at here: a phase is three rounds, so round 3 is a first phase that decided, and each later phase, rounds 6, 9 and on, is one more phase this seat ran undecided, on the coin or a phase behind a seat that decided first. A split run by hand shows it (`-S`): the split agreement decides phases past the first while every other decides at round 3. An operator deploys at n > 3t and reads this position after position; decisions past the first phase are agreements split, by a network slow past patience or by an adversary, and the answer is seats, since a split agreement's phases fit the byte only once n is above about 1.8 t^2 + t, as "Patience and phases" says.

What a seat sees of misbehavior it counts by class, seat-wide: units refused at each of cryptoMule's checks, a sign message under a second value, a row from a seat that is not the instance's initiator, a signing offset spent on a second value, and an old signature re-dealt.

The last two are refused, as cryptoMule refuses them, at or past the last position whose subset took the signer, and are judged against the signer's high water, and it moves only at a fold, for the members of the subset, never at a part's arrival: a part may come through another seat's hands under a position's name that seat chose, and only the subset is the same at every correct seat that folds the position. A position's name is the link before it, so a seat judges a position's parts only once it has folded every position before it, and the high water it judges them against, and so the refusal, is the same at every correct seat. The harness signs a dealer's second payload at its first payload's signing offset (`-f 11`): once the first is agreed, the second is refused at every seat and is in no subset. The message report names the member whose commitment was not its message's honest encoding, with the parts off it by recipient.

The windows are counted too, because they are the only view a seat has of where it stands: no report says another seat's position, so the rows that name a record here, the rows held under a name not yet minted, and the positions this seat DISCOVERed before its own agreement there completed, which is this seat behind the others, are how an operator sees a seat returning, ahead or behind, and the retention's measure is how it sees whether the declared retention is holding them; `acCounters` in `ac.h` names each count.

Every count of what a row cost or broke is kept per seat as well, behind the seat-wide counters and read one seat at a time (`acSeatCounters` in `ac.h`). The reason is cryptoMule's message attribution: identity on the wire is the in-tag sender the dealt pairwise tier verified, so a row that is malformed, that names an instance its sender does not initiate, that fails a gate or that re-sends what is held already is that sender's, and what a verified signature says, a second value at one signing offset, an old signature re-dealt, a sign message under another apex, is the signer's, whoever carried it.

The three windows are per seat the same way: the tick a seat's rows last landed in a live machine, last named a record, and were last held under a name not yet minted here; the newest of the three is where that seat is as this seat last saw it, and nothing else here says, since no seat reads another's position from anything but the rows it is given.

No callback reports another seat's position: the reports and the counters are the whole instrument, and the harness's judge reads the table the way an operator would, expecting the cut seat's rows to have named records and recover legs to have started toward it from them, under a dealt fault every refused part to be the dealer's, since the checks are cryptoMule's and no honest part fails one, and under a Byzantine row sender every fault counted to be in its row and the honest rows of faults at zero, its rows under names nobody minted held and the rest not held, and the record it names counted, which costs each seat a start of recover legs toward it for each row of it landing past their count; the sweeps' re-sends to a seat that has said HOLDING of nothing are counted in its row too, beside those to every seat, and are read, not judged. It asserts nothing of the other seats: under unbounded latency any seat may lag another by the live window, and up to t of them may do anything.

## What is tested

The harness attacks its own seats through the test build's hooks, [`acTest.h`](acTest.h): each message as it leaves and as it arrives, which the cuts and the declared network act on, and the dealer's fault and lag; the deployment build compiles none of them in. The attacks:

- a laggard whose new proposal the harness holds back a number of ticks, so it delivers only by presenting again on the same bytes what a subset left out, across a switch of signing context too, while it lets every other one go (`-z`, `-s`)
- a seat that proposes nothing, its positions launched on the others' rows with its noop as its input (`-i`)
- a seat cut off and recovered (`-x`)
- one cut off past the others' machines and carried by their recover legs a position a round trip (`-x` with a chain the abandon outlasts)
- one with nothing to say, cut while the chain idles at a folded head and the position after it then runs past the others' windows, so that on its return only the others' live rows under names it has not minted move it (`-i` with `-Z`, `-y` and `-x`)
- one frozen as a stopped process is, its clock and its sweeps standing still and its socket's queue filling and then losing, until every other seat has folded a given number of positions past it, an event the harness sees in their reports and never a time, so the others are past their live windows at the thaw whatever the host, with a receive buffer as small as a smaller host's system gives a socket, so that what arrived while it was stopped is lost as on a host whose socket buffer is that small (`-Q`, `-G`)
- one dead for the whole run (`-x` at the tick cap)
- one healed on ingress long before egress, recovered on the others' re-sends alone (`-X`)
- the whole network dark, from the go and from a tick after it (`-Y`, `-y`)
- the Internet's directed links coming and going, from four seats that hear nobody, through one-way links between pairs and a pair that hears both ways, then every link at once, the seats never told, and three seats that hear each other while the fourth is dark past their live windows, then hears them and is not heard, then is heard (`-T`)
- a dealer that sends one seat nothing of its own while the exchange toward that seat is dark (`-C`, `-U`)
- a scheduler that splits one agreement's inputs by withholding the readies of one value from two seats past patience, which the coins alone decide (`-S`)
- a short signing context forcing switches (`-s`)
- loss (`-D`), delay (`-L`) and loss in bursts (`-B`)
- a Byzantine row sender, one seat whose tags are rewritten at its egress and authenticated as its own word, saying HOLDING of nothing, naming its agreement rows at random, and naming a record in its own broadcast (`-b`)
- the same seat forwarding hostile bytes under its own key, a byte of every exchange part flipped or every unit's part a byte short, or a byte of every description beside its recover legs flipped, a lie about the subset, one of two faulty seats beside a returner (`-b` again, with `-2`)
- two faulty seats at once, one cut and one Byzantine, where the bound allows two (`-2`)
- a retention small enough to wrap, the oldest records let go as a chain slowed by loss outlasts the abandon, with a seat recovering across the wrap (`-M`, `-D`)
- a seat cut past the retention itself, partitioned, which the judge reads from the reports as an operator would, the position it is at let go by every other seat (`-x` past `-M`)
- a dealer that commits one of eleven faults (`-f`): six of cryptoMule's dealer faults (its `distribute.c`: shard, share, geom, ct, salt and pad), a flipped shard and a flipped share on two leaves, the two-faced and split signings and the apex signed at a second signing offset, all above, and a payload signed at its first payload's signing offset
- beside the salted dealer its accomplice, the second faulty seat, whose part is one of the T good ones and which forwards it to one seat alone (`-a`)
- a member's own shard landing after its position left the live window (`-U` with `-V`)
- this seat's memory refusing its search of a member until the position's end (`-O`)
- the end, called by every seat at a different link (`-e`) or by t seats alone (`-E`), and the next chain after it, from its final hash under a fresh deal, the seat dead for the first back in it (`-R`)

`make check` runs the ones that are this tree's, each under a comment in the [`Makefile`](Makefile) that says what it checks, since what each seat delivers of a dealer's message is how this tree puts the agreement and the dispersal together, which neither library sees. The other dealer's faults, the Byzantine row sender's other rewrites, the split agreement and the wider fault budgets test the agreement library and cryptoMule, which carry their own tests; the harness keeps them for a run by hand. The late shard and the memory refused are kept for a run by hand too.

Every run says what it expects of the dealer's message, and the judge fails it on anything else:

- a member's message at one correct seat and not at another, or not the same bytes
- a link that is not the fold of its own report, the head, the subset and the members' hashes, or a head that is not the link before it, the dealt genesis at position 1
- any of the counters it judges that a correct run should leave at zero
- a declared loss whose decodes never used a parity shard, or, in bursts, whose framers evicted no train either
- a laggard held past patience or a dead seat that no report entered 0 for
- a split agreement where every agreement decided at round 3
- a seat cut or frozen with the chain past the live window and no recover leg started from a record, or no position DISCOVERed there
- a freeze the chain was too short to carry past the live window, the arm not run
- a Byzantine row sender whose rewrites never fired, whose random names or record names did not reach its row of the table, or whose INITIAL under the genesis moved a subset
- hostile parts none of which was refused, or an honest seat's part refused
- hostile descriptions none of which was refused
- a partitioned seat whose rows were not held under the name nobody can place
- a declared retention that let no record go
- seats whose chains end at different positions or final hashes, an end with t or fewer members carrying it or a link past one with more, or a seat that did not stop on its own
- a next chain whose position 1 is not headed by the final hash at every seat, or whose subsets all leave out the seat dead in the first
- a row re-sent under a tick from its position's launch
- an honest dealer's message that took the search more than 4N tries, where one polynomial and one message take about a try a part
- an egress and ingress count that do not reconcile when nothing was lost or left in flight, the kernel's losses counted with the rest
